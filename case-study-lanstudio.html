The Beginning: Identifying the User & Market Gap

LAN Studio was born from a personal need for home decor that balanced accessibility and artistry. The market audit revealed a clear gap: consumers were forced to choose between cheap, impersonal, mass-produced items and luxury pieces with prohibitive prices.

This wasn't just a business opportunity; it was a user experience problem. The target audience—aesthetically discerning individuals who value authenticity and emotional warmth—had no online destination that made them feel understood. They struggled to find pieces that told a story and made their space truly feel like their own. This foundational insight was the catalyst for orchestrating the entire product strategy.



Market analysis revealing the white space between mass-produced and luxury home decor

Research & Synthesis: Defining the Wabi-Sabi User

Competitive Audit Insights

A comprehensive analysis of competitors showed they competed on either price or prestige, but rarely on emotional connection or artistic integrity. We plotted them on a matrix to visualize why our target user had no satisfying destination—they were forced to choose between authenticity and accessibility.

Analysis of LAN Studio's early web and gap between mass-produced and luxury home decor

By identifying this white space, we gained a validated strategic mandate to architect an experience that was both emotionally resonant (through storytelling and curation) and intuitively accessible (through streamlined navigation and mid-tier pricing).

User Pain Points

The Authenticity Seeker

"I want my home to tell a story, not just follow trends."

  • Frustrated by mass-produced items lacking craftsmanship
  • Seeks emotional connection with their possessions
  • Values sustainability and artisanal quality

The Mindful Curator

"My home is my sanctuary—every piece should bring calm."

  • Overwhelmed by cluttered, fast-paced shopping experiences
  • Prioritizes intentionality over impulse buying
  • Seeks brands aligned with personal values

The UX Insight

Our user wasn't just buying a product; they were investing in self-care, creativity, and a sense of calm. The brand positioning of 'affordable yet exceptional-quality pieces' was a direct UX requirement to solve these pain points. Our mission was to build trust where it was lacking.

Architecting the Experience: The Wabi-Sabi User Flow

The Problem

A traditional, overwhelming "Shop All" approach would contradict the brand's serene, curated philosophy and create decision fatigue for users seeking intentional purchases.

The Solution: A Curated, Intentional IA

To combat decision fatigue, I architected a streamlined user flow that moved away from a massive inventory dump. Instead, we designed the site around themed collections (Textural Ceramics, Handcrafted Accessories) that told a story and mirrored the wabi-sabi principles.

This halved the number of clicks to find a resonant item by guiding users through a validated, intuitive discovery journey, much like walking through a well-curated gallery rather than a crowded warehouse.


LAN Studio's systematic menu and information architecture

Building Trust Through Content

Each product page was treated as a destination. We integrated the founder's story and details on traditional craftsmanship directly into the layout, using UX writing to highlight the 'thoughtful materials' and 'emotional resonance' of each piece. This transformed the product description from a spec sheet into a trust-building narrative.

Validation & Iteration: Weaving in Customer Feedback

Usability Testing & Feedback Loops

Our customer service philosophy—thoughtful, attentive, and human-centered—became a core part of our research arm. We treated every client interaction as qualitative data, creating a continuous feedback loop for improvement.

Key Iterations Based on User Feedback

1

Clarifying Scale & Texture

Initial feedback indicated that users loved the product stories but were unsure about scale and texture. We iterated on the product pages by refining the microcopy to be more descriptive and adding photos with common objects for scale. This direct user feedback boosted conversion rates and reduced pre-purchase queries.

2

Mobile Engagement Boost

We noticed mobile users were less engaged. A quick usability test revealed the 'Add to Cart' button was often lost. We implemented a sticky CTA on mobile, which significantly boosted mobile engagement and made the path to purchase frictionless.

3

Streamlined Collection Navigation

Users expressed feeling overwhelmed by too many options. We refined our collection architecture to focus on curated themes rather than comprehensive categories, creating a more guided and serene discovery experience.

The Final Experience

After multiple iterations informed by user feedback and usability testing, we arrived at a final design that perfectly balanced the wabi-sabi aesthetic with e-commerce functionality.

Key Learnings

1

Brand is UX

The wabi-sabi philosophy wasn't just a marketing tagline; it was a design principle that directly informed the information architecture, visual design, and microcopy. Every design decision had to reflect serenity, authenticity, and intention.

2

Your Customer Service is a Usability Lab

The most candid feedback comes post-purchase. By being 'responsive and transparent,' we uncovered invaluable insights that drove iterative improvements and built lasting customer relationships.

3

Vision Guides, User Directs

As a founder, your vision is your guide, but the user is your compass. Validating the emotional needs of the user was what transformed a store into a destination and transactions into relationships.

LAN Studio taught me that entrepreneurship and UX design are deeply intertwined. Building a brand from scratch forced me to consider every touchpoint through a user-centered lens, from the first website visit to the unboxing experience. This holistic approach to UX—where business strategy and user needs align—is a perspective I'll carry forward to all future projects.
